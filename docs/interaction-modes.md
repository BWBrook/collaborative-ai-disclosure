# Interaction modes: descriptive, overlapping and unranked

**Status:** four working categories and the predominant-plus-overlap architecture emerged from the founding discussion. Definitions remain open to refinement and testing on real projects.

## AI assistance

The human predominantly conducts the research and calls on AI for bounded support. For example, an agent might help diagnose a particular software error or revise a passage, with little sustained participation in the project's conceptual development.

“Predominantly” is descriptive, not a measured labour threshold. The important feature is the bounded relationship, not whether a stopwatch shows 49% or 51% AI work.

## Governed human–AI collaboration

The human and AI are recurrently embedded in developing the research. An agent may perform much of the implementation while humans contribute direction, domain knowledge, criticism, judgement and integration throughout. Ideas and wording can be co-developed rather than exchanged as finished modules.

This describes Barry's reported current model. It does not imply human approval of every command, or guarantee that every project described this way has adequate oversight. The narrative must give the term content.

## Delegated scientific problem-solving

A human supplies a substantive scientific question or problem, possibly including data, prior attempts and constraints. The agent largely determines and executes the route towards a solution, returning results for evaluation. Recurrent human involvement in intermediate reasoning may be less extensive than in governed collaboration.

This is not “ungoverned” by definition. A delegated project still needs an account of authorisation, evaluation and responsibility. It is not evidence that any particular current system can reliably complete a specified research programme autonomously.

## Agent-led exploration

A human supplies a broad field, resource or opportunity, while the agent substantially identifies questions and pursues investigations. The founding example was a long-running camera-trap dataset supplied with an invitation to find worthwhile scientific questions, rather than a sharply specified hypothesis.

This is a conceptual category, not a claim of demonstrated end-to-end capability or permission under any publisher's policy. Data stewardship, authorship eligibility and the sufficiency of later evaluation remain separate questions.

## How to use the categories

Lead with the best overall descriptor, then explain meaningful overlaps or changes:

> The project predominantly involved governed human–AI collaboration. Within that relationship, implementation of several defined numerical comparisons was delegated, whereas figure labelling received bounded AI assistance.

This sentence classifies a relationship; it does not allocate exclusive ownership of ideas, code or text. A concise explanation of what the humans and AI actually did should follow.

For a project with no defensible predominant mode, say that explicitly and describe the mixture. This escape clause is a scaffold implementation proposal, not a separate agreement recorded in the conversation. Its purpose is to preserve truthfulness rather than force a label.

## What the categories do not measure

They do not measure scientific quality, trustworthiness, labour percentages, amount of text generated, compute spent, author eligibility, or the evidential strength of results. They are not ordered levels from “basic” to “advanced”.

## Optional vocabulary

Researchers can mention question refinement, literature discovery, data sourcing, coding, modelling, diagnostics, interpretation, criticism, visualisation and manuscript development. These are examples, not compulsory fields. More than one participant may be involved in all of them.

A statement that AI was embedded throughout is a legitimate starting description. It becomes more informative when accompanied by a few consequential examples or an explanation of how direction and evaluation operated. “Everything was collaborative” should not become a replacement for giving any substantive account.
