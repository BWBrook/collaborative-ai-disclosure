# Positioning among existing disclosure frameworks

**Proposed synthesis, 23 October 2026.** This comparison informs development; it is not an adopted principle or a systematic review. The [source register](../references/source-register.json) records versions, who inspected them, and exclusions. The main texts of all six frameworks below were read; for some PDFs this means complete extractable text, with uninspected screenshots or diagrams noted in the register. Supplementary coverage varies by source.

Existing frameworks already recognise intellectual AI involvement, workflow documentation and checking. Our case cannot rest on portraying them collectively as lists of delegated tasks. The question is whether this project's narrative default and account of how claims were checked make intertwined research easier to describe truthfully.

GAIDeT's published template builds in an assurance: the [article's](https://doi.org/10.1080/08989621.2025.2544331) section 5.2.1 sample declaration asserts full human supervision and excludes AI authorship, consistent with its section 4.1 rationale. The inspected [generator](https://github.com/panbibliotekar/gaidet-declaration/blob/7f21a877c7fbdb3f35bc0da2e2b156e6e70b2551/index.html) inserts both assertions without collecting evidence for them; local calls with empty and illustrative inputs confirm that behaviour. This implements the published template's design. The assertions still need support in the particular research account, and copied declarations can be edited. This project's proposed templates should elicit what supervision and checking occurred, including their limits.

The [EQUATOR listing](https://www.equator-network.org/library/reporting-guidelines-under-development/reporting-guidelines-under-development-for-other-study-designs/#CANGARU) still places CANGARU under development. A targeted search on 23 October 2026 did not locate a final consensus checklist; the listing's old projected publication date is not evidence that it was completed.

## What we can defensibly offer

The established project direction combines a workflow narrative, overlapping interaction modes, optional finer provenance and scientific governance described by function. It accommodates repeated joint reasoning without requiring exclusive ownership of each activity or a numerical labour allocation. These are design choices to make a particular kind of research legible. Their combination may prove useful without being unprecedented.

The [P06 principle](principles.md) makes the warrant for a contribution central while retaining origin as information about necessary checks. The proposed operational account in [warrant and independence](warrant-and-independence.md) is an application and synthesis of existing ideas. [Lawlor, Tilling and Davey Smith](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw314) provide an earlier account of triangulation through approaches with different biases; [Mayo](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811076/64647/excerpt/9781107664647_excerpt.pdf) develops the requirement that a test be capable of exposing a claim's flaws. Only selected passages of these sources were inspected.

There is also close AI-specific prior art. [Nascimento and da Silva](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01330-w), section 4.1, already connect independent evidence with checks capable of detecting relevant errors. [Hsieh and Chen](https://doi.org/10.2196/106068) distinguish disclosure from claim defensibility and connect claims to evidence, interpretation and uncertainty. Selected passages of both were read. Neither the general move towards warrant nor the independence/detection argument should be presented as this project's discovery.

A practical contribution would be to help authors explain, economically, which consequential claims were checked, which failures the checks addressed, what dependencies remained, what failed and how the claim changed. Whether our proposed records achieve that is still open. A schema can preserve an answer to those questions; it cannot establish that the answer is true or the check adequate.

## The comparison that should decide further development

Apply the competing formats to the same documented research episodes: a bounded editing task, an iterative joint analysis and an agent-led exploration with failed checks. Ask readers to identify consequential AI involvement, remaining uncertainty and the evidence bearing on the claims, then compare author effort and omitted or distorted information. Preserve useful mappings to existing schemes.

If an existing framework conveys the same account with less work, adapt it. If our narrative hides details that a structured prompt exposes, add the prompt. This project needs a demonstrable reporting benefit, not a claim that earlier authors overlooked collaboration or verification.
