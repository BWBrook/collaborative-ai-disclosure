# Scope and non-goals

This project develops language and practices for honest, useful disclosure of AI participation in scientific research. It is not an AI-use detector, an authorship-percentage calculator, a moral ranking of research workflows, a universal definition of intelligence, or a substitute for scientific peer review.

It does not assume that more AI produces better science, that every researcher should use AI, or that every type of research can appropriately adopt the same relationship. It must accommodate people who use only bounded assistance without treating them as less advanced.

It does not certify compliance with journal policies, settle formal authorship eligibility, override confidentiality obligations, or promise that public transcripts are safe to release. It does not require access to proprietary systems or logs as a precondition for an honest disclosure.

Its scope includes proposals, papers, datasets, software and other research artifacts where the framework is useful. The first templates focus on journal articles and reproducibility repositories. Reviewer/editor policies and regulated research need their own contextual treatment, not an automatic extension of author-facing examples.

Predictions of increasingly autonomous scientific agents are background motivation, not established capability claims. No timeline from the founding conversation is treated as a forecast to publish as fact.

Public participation is an aspiration. Workshop interest reported by the founder is not formal validation, a consensus statement, institutional endorsement or evidence that the framework already has a community of maintainers.
