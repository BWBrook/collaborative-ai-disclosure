# Working principles

**Status:** principles P01–P05 reflect the direction established in the founding discussion. Their wording below is a scaffold synthesis, not a verbatim ratified policy. P06 is unresolved. See the [decision log](../provenance/decision-log.md).

## P01 — The workflow is the primary unit of disclosure

For intertwined human–AI research, describe the collaborative workflow rather than manufacturing sentence-by-sentence, line-by-line or idea-by-idea ownership. Preserve more detailed provenance where practicable and appropriate; its relevance depends on the scientific and editorial question being asked.

This is not an exemption from specificity. A workflow narrative must still make consequential AI involvement intelligible. Nor does it mean that granular records are always uninteresting: a particular model-mediated classification, data-extraction procedure or disputed decision may require detailed inspection.

Three records may coexist: a clean release that reproduces the reported results; a development history showing iterations and reversals; and a deliberative record of the human–AI interaction. They are complementary, not interchangeable. Absence of older records must be reported honestly rather than repaired by invented precision.

## P02 — Describe the actual relationship

Use language that matches the mode of participation. “AI assistance” can accurately describe bounded support. “Human–AI collaboration” can more accurately describe iterative and substantive contributions to reasoning, methods, implementation, criticism, interpretation and communication.

Collaboration is used here as a functional description, not a claim about consciousness, moral agency, legal personhood, or entitlement to scientific authorship. Authorship requirements and publication responsibilities must be checked separately against the applicable policy; see the [policy register](../policies/README.md).

Do not claim that all ideas originated with humans when that did not happen. Equally, do not inflate incidental assistance into substantive collaboration. This framework is intended to make either account more accurate, not to reward maximal AI use.

## P03 — Describe human scientific contribution by function

Human contribution may consist of motivation, question-setting, knowledge of the system, methodological judgement, recurrent redirection, evaluation of evidence, synthesis, and responsibility for the claims advanced. It need not correspond to the proportion of operations performed personally.

No operational 50% boundary between assistance and governance was adopted in the founding discussion. Time, output volume and intellectual consequence are not interchangeable quantities. A low share of execution neither proves nor disproves meaningful governance.

Governance must nevertheless mean something substantive. A title, an initial instruction or nominal sign-off is not by itself evidence that consequential choices were understood and evaluated. The appropriate evidential account is a live design question, addressed provisionally under P06.

The principal-investigator/postdoctoral-supervision analogy is useful for distinguishing execution from direction. It is not an equivalence between an AI system and a human researcher, nor a substitute for evaluating the actual responsibilities involved.

## P04 — Use a predominant mode and acknowledge overlap

A clearly defined predominant mode can orient the reader while secondary modes describe departures, overlaps and changes through time. The four working descriptors are AI assistance, governed human–AI collaboration, delegated scientific problem-solving and agent-led exploration.

They describe relationships, not a quality ranking, mandatory progression or scale of research maturity. A project may change modes between phases or exhibit several simultaneously. Explain a mixed case in prose instead of forcing a misleading classification.

The framework retains defined categories but does not require numeric scores, percentages or a computational assignment algorithm. See [interaction modes](interaction-modes.md).

## P05 — Narrative first, optional role vocabulary

The default account should be narrative and holistic. Role terms can help where activities are genuinely separable or an external requirement demands detail, but they must not imply exclusive ownership of tasks or ideas.

Writing is a particularly important test case. Drafting, criticism and expression may change the scientific reasoning itself. Neither “human thinking, AI wording” nor “AI wrote it, therefore humans did not think” should be assumed. Explain the actual process.

A rigid role form may encourage artificial task separation or strategic claims that the human performed conventionally valued activities. This is a design risk the framework aims to avoid, not an empirical claim here that a particular existing taxonomy causes those behaviours.

The framework does not replace human-author contribution statements that a journal separately requires. Optional mappings can describe involvement without claiming exclusivity.

## Cross-cutting design rule

**Document authentic scientific practice; do not prescribe a workflow merely to make the disclosure easier to fill in.**

This rule does not waive research ethics, privacy duties, methodological standards or editorial restrictions. It prevents the template from quietly substituting its preferred division of labour for what actually occurred.
