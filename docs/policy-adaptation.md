# Adapting an honest account to a journal

**Status:** implementation guidance for review, not legal advice or an assurance that a submission complies with a particular policy.

## The research account comes first

Write what actually happened before adapting the wording to a journal form. A venue-specific statement should be a faithful view of that account, not a retrospective invention of a more acceptable division of work.

The framework's use of “collaboration” does not assign AI formal authorship. Current requirements relevant to the sources inspected are recorded with their scope and limitations in the [policy register](../policies/README.md). Do not extrapolate a rule from one journal to a whole publisher without support.

## Check the applicable layers

Read the target journal's current author instructions, the publisher policy it incorporates, the submission questionnaire and any applicable institutional, funder, data-use or ethics requirements. Distinguish rules governing authors from those governing reviewers and editors. Permission to use AI on one's own manuscript does not imply permission to upload another researcher's confidential submission.

Record the URL, date checked, stated update date if available, relevant clause or heading, required placement, reporting detail, and any unresolved ambiguity. The [policy-check template](../templates/policy-check.md) is optional working support.

## Translate detail, not reality

A narrative-first statement can include specific tool names, approximate dates, model versions when known, tasks and oversight when required. Detail does not require inventing separable ownership. For an embedded workflow, explain that functions were jointly developed while still describing substantive involvement.

A paper-level declaration does not replace method-specific documentation of an AI-dependent analytical step. Nor does complete disclosure make a prohibited practice permissible.

When a policy is restrictive, ambiguous or in tension with the actual workflow, state the issue plainly and seek clarification. Do not relabel substantive conceptual participation as copyediting, omit inconvenient tools, or assert that all ideas were human-originated to make the submission look compliant.

## Model and date uncertainty

Record what is known and how it is known. Use “not recorded” or a qualified date range rather than guessing. A product name, account tier, agent interface and underlying model identifier are different pieces of information. Exact historical backend identities may not be recoverable.

## Journal adapters

A future adapter may specify required placement and additional factual fields. It should not claim universal compatibility, auto-certify compliance, or quietly rewrite the contribution narrative. The initial repository intentionally provides a source register and a review process rather than pre-certified declarations for every publisher.

## Maintenance

A source check is an event, not an ongoing service. Retain previous interpretations in Git once the repository is versioned, record substantive changes, and re-check requirements near submission. Offline age warnings in this repository do not inspect the web or detect policy changes.
