# Example: delegation versus exploration

**HYPOTHETICAL DESIGN CASES. These are not claims that a present system completed the work described or that a journal would accept the workflow.**

## A specified problem

A scientist supplies a precise question about an observation model, a dataset, known failed approaches and constraints. The agent develops candidate solutions and runs comparisons with limited intermediate interaction. The scientist then evaluates the method and claims before any submission.

The working descriptor is **delegated scientific problem-solving**. The narrative should explain the initial intellectual input, the scope of delegation, the evaluation actually performed and unresolved limitations. Merely receiving the output is not evidence that it was adequately evaluated.

## An open-ended opportunity

A scientist supplies a long-running wildlife-monitoring dataset and a broad request to identify useful scientific questions. The agent proposes questions, explores analyses and drafts candidate interpretations.

The working descriptor is **agent-led exploration**. This is distinct from having supplied a sharp scientific problem. The eventual account would need to identify the origin of the questions and the scope of human evaluation. Data collection alone is not treated by this framework as automatically satisfying a journal's authorship criteria.

## A blended project

An exploratory phase may generate a question that becomes the subject of deeply governed collaboration. The final narrative could identify agent-led exploration as an early mode and governed collaboration as the predominant relationship in producing the eventual claims.

The categories should make the history clearer, not freeze the project at its initial instruction or imply that the lightest initial direction establishes adequate accountability throughout.
